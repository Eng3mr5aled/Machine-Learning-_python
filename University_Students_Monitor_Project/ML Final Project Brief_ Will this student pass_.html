Will this student pass?

ML course final project. You get one dataset and two questions. Answer both with the tools we learned in class.

Your job: act as a data scientist for a university and build models that predict a student's final score and whether they pass.

The scenario

The university wants an early-warning system. By week 8, the advisor knows how many hours a student studies, how often they attend, and a few other facts. Can we spot who will struggle before the exam?

Part A: how much?

Predict the exact final_score (0 to 100). This is regression.

Part B: yes or no?

Predict passed (1 if the score is 55 or more). This is classification.

The data: 500 students

File: student_performance.csv. A few prev_gpa and sleep_hours values are missing on purpose. Handle them and explain how.

Part A: predict the score

  • Fit a linear regression on all features.
  • Fit a polynomial regression. Hint: plot study_hours_week against final_score first. Does the 20th hour help as much as the 2nd?
  • Choose the polynomial degree with k-fold cross-validation, not by guessing.
  • Report RMSE and R² on the test set. Which model wins, and why?

Part B: predict pass or fail

Logistic regression

Tune the regularization strength C.

K-nearest neighbors

Tune k. Scale features first. Say why scaling matters here.

Decision tree

Tune max_depth. Show the tree and name the top split.

  • Report accuracy, precision, recall, F1 and a confusion matrix for each model.
  • About 30% of students fail. Explain why accuracy alone can mislead.

Rules of the method

  • Split train and test once, with a fixed random_state. Never tune on the test set.
  • Fit the scaler and any imputer on the training data only. Then apply them to the test data.
  • Use 5-fold cross-validation and GridSearchCV for every hyperparameter you tune.
  • Keep one table comparing all models on the same test set.
  • Finish with a recommendation: which model would you give the university, and what would you improve next?
